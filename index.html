Fun and inclusion rules for every game
  • Score the team by default. Team or co-op scoring beats individual rankings, so nobody is publicly last.
  • Offer a relaxed mode for anything timed. Time pressure rewards fast typists, not good thinkers.
  • Give everyone a way in. Talkers, planners, and doers each get a role.
  • No prior experience needed to start. Someone on day one can play alongside a ten-year veteran.
  • Keep failure low-stakes. Wrong answers should lead to a quick explanation and another try, never a public red mark.
  • Build it accessible. Never rely on color alone, caption any audio, and make it work with a keyboard.

Where these came from

The shelf is tuned for a technology company: every game here can carry product, sales, or implementation content, and generic icebreakers and team-building games were left out. It started with 30 formats and grew from what corporate L&D teams, facilitators, tool makers, and AI adoption programs describe using. No list is ever complete: facilitation books like Gamestorming hold 80+ more workshop techniques, many of them meeting methods rather than games. Cards link to the examples behind them.

    "Used recently" marks are saved in this browser only. Group sizes and times are starting points, not limits. Every game names a well-known game to use as a feel reference: "Inspired by" means the mechanic comes straight from that game, and "Feels like" means it's a good reference for pacing and feel. Describe what you want from playing the game, never by copying its code, art, or assets. the game names are their owners' trademarks, so build yours with its own name, theme, and art.
